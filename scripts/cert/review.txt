The repository is slicc-extension, one Chrome MV3 extension for two products: six (the hosted leader tab on www.sliccy.ai, through externally_connectable: the CDP bridge, the fetch proxy that injects secrets, the secrets page and the side panel) and seven (SLICC pages on *.sliccy.ai: DNR CORS rules, the sliccExtension.fetch relay and sliccExtension.cdp over chrome.debugger). It ships to Chrome Web Store users. The CI evidence, when present, holds the guard-vector rows of the pull request's own CI run (guard-rows) next to main's.

Certify against these rules (the cert lane's rules for this extension):
1. Protected origins (PRE-1, from the pinned @ai-ecoverse/slicc-browsers catalog): vault, vault-preview, vault-*, get, mcpcat and the sliccy.ai apex get nothing: no content scripts, no ports, no CDP, no relay. www.sliccy.ai gets six's surfaces and seven's relay; seven's labels get the relay and the CDP surface. Every port checks the sender's origin. A change that widens who reaches a port, a content script, the relay or CDP (matches, exclude_matches, exclude_globs, externally_connectable, trusted(), sender checks, the generated seven*.js and protected-rules.js inputs) is a fail unless it is deliberate, narrow and tested.
2. The CDP guard, on both transports: seven's sliccExtension.cdp (extension/cdp.js, extension/shield.js) and six's bridge (six/extension/bridge-sw.ts, cdp-proxy-sw.ts, cdp-shield.ts, protected-frames-sw.ts). Both must refuse protected targets on every path: attach, create and navigate (including redirects, popups and about:/view-source:/blob:/filesystem: wrappers), protected iframes, Fetch interception (no requestPaused or authRequired for protected URLs, no fulfil of a protected document), cookie and storage methods (refused or filtered), privileged pages (chrome:, chrome-extension: including other extensions and ours, devtools:, file:, browser_ui targets), and the pre-registered effects (init scripts, bindings, bypassCSP, breakpoints, auto-attach, session overrides). Execution contexts and worlds that belong to browser extensions must stay out of a client's reach. Weakening any of these on either transport, or fixing one transport and not the other, is a fail.
3. Internal messages and secrets (B-SEC1, B-SEC3): every internal onMessage handler requires sender.id === chrome.runtime.id and no sender.tab; secret values never reach a content script or a page; secrets stay encrypted records in IndexedDB; the fetch proxy injects secrets only for their scoped domains. A secret value that can leave the service worker, a log of a secret, or a gate that can be skipped is a fail.
4. CORS and the relay: the DNR rules (extension/rules.json) lift CORS only for requests initiated by sliccy.ai pages; the relay refuses protected targets. Widening the initiators or the targets is a fail.
5. Permissions and packaging (CWS): manifest permissions, optional permissions and host permissions; the manifest key (the store ID akjjllgokmbgpbdbmafpiefnhidlmbgf must not change); minimum_chrome_version; scripts/pack.mjs ships only extension/, no sources or source maps, no _metadata/; the release gates in scripts/ (check-live-permissions, check-six-floor, cws-publish's version check, gate.mjs's PASS lines) must not be weakened. A new permission or install warning is a fail unless the same change updates the live-permissions gate's baseline deliberately and narrowly.
6. Build and supply chain: the build stays local and byte-reproducible (no fetching at build time), dependencies stay exact-pinned, a new dependency in dependencies (not devDependencies) is bundled into users' extension and needs a reason, workflows that release or publish must not gain a new path to the store or to secrets.
